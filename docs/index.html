Which method catches a sick digester first?

An agricultural biogas plant (maize silage and cattle slurry, about 0.5 MWel) simulated with ADM1 in its BSM2 implementation, verified against the published BSM2 steady state. Seven kinds of upset are injected with known ground truth - five process upsets and two sensor faults - and eight monitoring methods watch the plant through realistic sensors: online gas flow, CH4, pH and temperature with noise, drift and gaps, and a FOS/TAC titration twice a week. Everything below is measured on 140 upsets and three normal years that no method saw while it was tuned, and every detection rate is shown next to the rate the method's own false alarms reach by chance. Code and method: github.com/kwwaleszczynski-del/biogas-digester-twin.

2. Method comparison

Process upsets: overload, shock load, ammonia, heating failure, substrate change. Sensor faults: drifting CH4 analyser, frozen gas flow meter. One event rule for everything: an alarm onset is a day in alarm after a day without. Detected: an onset within 14 days from the cause. Chance: the same rule applied at every date of normal operation, where any onset is a coincidence; excess = detected minus chance. False alarms: onsets per year of normal operation; days in alarm: the share of normal days a method is in alarm. Ammonia, before loss: share of the ammonia upsets alarmed before their yield loss began (the one upset type whose loss builds up after the cause). Brackets: 95 % bootstrap intervals over test runs.

Left: each method at its own threshold; the open circle is its chance level at the same false-alarm habit. Right: every detector's score threshold moved until it raises at most 2 false alarms a year on the test runs (lab 10 % missing), read off the curves: a fair comparison of how well each method separates upsets from normal operation, not a threshold a plant could set in advance.

Two checks (lab 10 % missing): gradient boosting without the feed log (what and how much was fed), and the EKF twin with its model parameters off by about 20 % and re-zeroed on each run's first weeks.

5. Real farm data: false alarms

Four small manure-fed digesters monitored daily for a year (Mulat, ILRI, Mendeley Data, CC BY 4.0; weekly means shown). No pH or FOS/TAC was logged, so only the gas-based detectors apply, each calibrated on the digester's own first eight weeks after start-up (shaded). Red triangles are weeks with an operator note about a repair, a closed valve or the meter (only the two flexible-bag units carry notes).
