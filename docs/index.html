Which method catches a sick digester first?

An agricultural biogas plant (maize silage and cattle slurry, about 0.5 MWel) simulated with ADM1 in its BSM2 implementation, verified against the published BSM2 steady state. Seven kinds of upset are injected with known ground truth - five process upsets and two sensor faults - and eight monitoring methods watch the plant through realistic sensors: online gas flow, CH4, pH and temperature with noise, drift and gaps, and a FOS/TAC titration twice a week. Everything below is measured on 140 upsets and three normal years that no method saw while it was tuned. Code and method: github.com/kwwaleszczynski-del/biogas-digester-twin.

2. Method comparison

Process upsets: overload, shock load, ammonia, heating failure, substrate change. Sensor faults: drifting CH4 analyser, frozen gas flow meter. A detection is an alarm that starts within 60 days after the cause; false alarms are alarm episodes in normal operation. Before yield loss: share of the process upsets that went on to lose yield and were alarmed before the loss began.

5. Real farm data: false alarms

Four small manure-fed digesters monitored daily for a year (Mulat, ILRI, Mendeley Data, CC BY 4.0; weekly means shown). No pH or FOS/TAC was logged, so only the gas-based detectors apply, each calibrated on the digester's own first eight weeks after start-up (shaded).
